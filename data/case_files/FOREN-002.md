GARDA TECHNICAL BUREAU. EXAMINATION REPORT.
Incident ref: DMR-26-10418
Examiner: Dr. Eoin Carr, Crime Scene Examiner
Requested by: Det. Garda Sinéad Walsh
Dates of examination: 2026-10-04 (13:45 to 16:30) and 2026-10-07 (clock test)
Report date: 2026-10-08

1. SCOPE
I was asked to examine the east loading door (DOOR-E2), the east corridor (EAST-CORR) and the rear car park of the Liffey Innovation Hub, and to check the timing of the access control system against the CCTV system.

2. DOOR-E2 AND THE BADGE READER
DOOR-E2 is a single steel fire and loading door with a self-closer and a magnetic lock. Entry from outside needs a valid badge on the reader. Exit from inside is by a green push-to-exit (REX) button. A REX exit does not record any badge. The door showed no damage or tampering. The self-closer works: in ten tests the door closed and locked within 4 to 6 seconds of being released. The door cannot be left propped open without triggering a "door held" alarm after 30 seconds, and the system recorded no such alarm on the night in question.

The reader was tested with a known test badge in the presence of the facilities manager, Brian Coyle. It reads correctly and records each event.

3. CLOCK SYNCHRONISATION
The access control system and the CCTV system have separate clocks. On 2026-10-07 I triggered a test badge event at DOOR-E2 while it was in view of CAM-03 and compared the two time stamps, five times.
- Result: the DOOR-E2 reader clock is 6 seconds AHEAD of the CCTV system clock (range 5.8 to 6.3 seconds).
- Facilities confirm that no clock adjustment was made between 2026-10-03 and 2026-10-07.
- So an access event logged at, for example, 01:43:12 on the reader corresponds to about 01:43:06 on CCTV.
The CAM-01 and DOOR-M1 pair was checked the same way; there the offset is under 1 second.

4. EAST CORRIDOR FLOOR
The corridor floor is vinyl. The cleaner mopped it at about 01:32 to 01:36 on the night (per the CCTV review). The facilities team did not clean it again before Garda attendance, and the corridor was taped off from 08:15. Using oblique lighting I recovered partial dried wet-footwear marks on the corridor floor between DOOR-E2 and the east entrance of the main hall.
- At least three different sole patterns are present.
- Pattern A: a trainer-type sole, about UK size 9, consistent with the footwear of the participant who went out to collect a delivery.
- Pattern B: a flat-soled work shoe, about UK size 5, consistent with the cleaner's footwear.
- Pattern C: a smooth leather sole with a separate heel block, about UK size 11, typical of a men's formal or smart-casual shoe. Pattern C runs from DOOR-E2 to the hall entrance and back. It is not consistent with any of the footwear examined so far.
These marks were made on a wet surface, so they were made after the corridor was mopped and while people were coming in from the rain.

5. REAR CAR PARK
Bay P-31 is in the far north-east corner, next to the bins and the skip. It is not lit; the nearest floodlight is about 25 m away. Rain had washed the tarmac, and no useful tyre or footwear impressions were recovered from the bay.

The skip was examined on 2026-10-04 at 09:40 with the facilities manager. It contained pizza boxes, flattened cardboard, food waste and drinks cans. No laptop, grey hard case or electronic items were found. This matches the cleaner's account of taking out the hall waste at about 02:14.

6. CONCLUSIONS
(a) DOOR-E2 and its reader were working normally, with no tampering, and the door was not propped open.
(b) The DOOR-E2 reader clock runs 6 seconds ahead of CCTV. Access times must be shifted back by 6 seconds to match CAM-03.
(c) A smooth-soled men's shoe, about UK size 11 (pattern C), walked from DOOR-E2 to the main hall and back after the corridor was mopped at about 01:32. It does not match the footwear of the participant or the cleaner.
(d) The skip contained no stolen property.
